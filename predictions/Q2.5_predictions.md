# Q2.5 — Predictions before Testing the Baseline

Written before running any mirror, rotation or speed experiment on model A.
Inputs used: Table 2, Figure 2, Figure 1 (median speed 3.32 px/frame, all
directions present) and the reach estimate from Q2.3 (about 8 px per step).

**Mirror.** Model A will mostly pass the mirror test. Mirroring maps a motion
direction θ to 180° − θ, and Figure 1(a) shows that every direction already
occurs in training, so the mirrored motion is familiar. What changes is the
digits' appearance: a mirrored 2 or 7 is not a shape the model has seen. I
expect E_M to be somewhat higher than E(5) (roughly 10–30%), with a small but
non-zero consistency gap C_M. The extra error will show up as blurrier or
distorted digits, rather than digits moving the wrong way.

**Rotation.** Rotation will be harder than mirroring, but it will also not
fail completely. A 90° rotation shifts every direction by 90°, which is again
a familiar motion, so the motion rule should still carry over. But rotated
digits look even less like training digits than mirrored ones, so I expect
E_R > E_M and C_R > C_M. If rotation is worse, I would attribute the
difference to appearance, not motion.

**Speed.** At 1× (about 3.3 px per frame) digits move well within the model's
reach of about 8 px per step. At 2× (about 6.6 px) the motion is still inside
the reach but faster than almost anything in training (the 99th percentile is
4.7 px), so I expect clear degradation, mainly extra blur and lag, but the
digits should still roughly follow their paths. At 3× (about 10 px) digits
jump further than information can travel in one step, so model A will break
down: I expect digits to stall near their last position, smear out, or fade
away, with E(2) at 3× close to or worse than Copy-last.
